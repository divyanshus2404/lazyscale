# The first three clients

You have no case studies, no testimonials and no real numbers. Everything on your site
is a claim. Three free builds fix that — if they are run properly.

Run carelessly, free work becomes unpaid support forever, and you finish with three
happy users and still nothing to show a prospect. The difference is entirely in how you
set it up before you start.

**Stagger them.** Do the first, learn from it, then start the second. Three at once
means making the same mistake three times.

---

## Never use the word "free"

"Free" invites people to treat it as worthless and to treat your time as unlimited. It
also devalues the ₹4,999 you want to charge the next person.

Say **founding client**. Name the real price, then discount it visibly to zero, and be
explicit about what you want in exchange. You are trading, not begging.

> "I'm taking on three founding clients. Normally ₹4,999 a month — for these three
> it's free for the first month, because what I want in return is your numbers and a
> testimonial I can put on the site."

That sentence does four jobs: anchors the price, explains why it costs nothing, states
what you want back, and implies scarcity that happens to be true.

---

## Who to pick

**Good:**
- The pain recurs weekly or daily, and they complain about it unprompted
- They already own the tools — no migration first
- Responsive. If they take four days to reply now, delivery will take a month
- Recognisable enough that naming them means something
- Willing to tell you real numbers

**Avoid, however tempting:**
- Close friends who will be too polite to tell you it is not working
- Businesses with chaotic or non-existent data
- Anyone who wants three workflows before you have delivered one
- Anyone who cannot name a single number about their own process
- Pre-launch startups with no real volume — nothing to automate yet

Pick businesses whose logo you would want on your site. You are choosing your first
three case studies, not doing favours.

---

## Measure before you touch anything

**This is the step that decides whether you get a case study at all.** You cannot write
"saved 6 hours a week" if you never established what the before was.

Ask, and write down, before any build:

- How many of these come in a week?
- Who handles them, and roughly how long each one takes?
- How quickly do you respond today, honestly? Best case and typical case.
- How many slip through completely?
- What does an hour of that person's time cost?

If they do not know, have them count for one week before you start. That week is not a
delay — it is the first half of your case study, and asking for it makes you look
rigorous rather than eager.

---

## What you commit to, in writing

Send this as an email before starting. It is not a contract, it is a boundary — and it
is what stops month one becoming month six.

> **What I'll do**
> - Build one workflow: [specific description in four steps]
> - Live within [X] days of getting access
> - Fix anything broken in what I built, for 30 days
>
> **What I need from you**
> - Access to [tools], and your numbers from the week before we start
> - Feedback within 2 working days when I ask
> - A short testimonial at day 30, and permission to describe the results publicly
>   — naming you if you're comfortable, anonymised as "a Bangalore logistics firm"
>   if not
>
> **What happens at day 30**
> Either you keep it running at ₹[X] a month, or I hand it over documented and we
> part on good terms. No obligation either way.

**One workflow. Thirty days. Testimonial agreed up front.**

Agreeing the testimonial at the end never works — they are busy, the urgency is gone,
and you feel awkward asking. Agree it before you start, when they are enthusiastic and
it costs them nothing.

## Outreach

### Someone you know

> Hey [name] — I've started building AI automations for startups, mostly lead response
> and WhatsApp.
>
> I'm looking for three founding clients to build for at no cost, because I want real
> results I can point at. You mentioned [the specific thing they complained about] —
> that's exactly the shape of thing I'd automate.
>
> Roughly: [one sentence on what it would do]. Takes me a couple of days.
>
> All I'd want back is your before-and-after numbers and a few lines I can quote.
>
> Worth 15 minutes?

Specific beats broad. "You mentioned you're drowning in WhatsApp enquiries" gets a
reply; "let me know if you need automation" does not.

### Posting in a community

Do not post an ad. Post the work.

Run your own audit tool on a real, common problem and publish the output, with the
workflow you would build. Then one line at the end: *"I'm doing three of these free at
the moment — comment or DM if it's useful."*

Show the thing. People scroll past offers and stop for something useful.

### If they go quiet

One follow-up after four days, then stop.

> Hey [name], still keen to do this one if it's useful — happy to park it if the
> timing's wrong. Either's fine.

Never chase twice. It reads as desperate and it is not where your next client is.

## Deliver it like a paid project

The whole point is learning what paid delivery feels like. Follow the sequence in
`README.md` exactly — especially **testing with their real historical data** and the
**2–3 day shadow run** where everything drafts and nothing sends.

Free clients are where you *should* be strictest, because a mistake here costs you a
testimonial rather than a paying customer.

Keep notes on everything that breaks. That list becomes your pre-launch checklist, and
it is worth more than the case study.

---

## The case study

Write it within a week of finishing, while the numbers are fresh.

```
[Business type, city] — [what you automated]

THE PROBLEM
Two or three sentences. What was happening, who was doing it, what it cost.
Concrete: "Priya checked WhatsApp twice a day and roughly a third of enquiries
never got a reply."

WHAT WE BUILT
Four steps, plain language, naming the actual tools.

THE RESULT
Before → after. Response time. Hours per week. Anything missed.
Use real numbers or none at all.

WHAT THEY SAID
Two or three lines, in their words.

Built in [X] days. Running since [month].
```

**Never invent or round up a number.** One honest "cut first response from 6 hours to
under 2 minutes" beats any amount of "10x faster". And a prospect who checks with the
named client and finds it accurate is a prospect who signs.

## Getting the testimonial

At day 30, ask specifically. "Any feedback?" produces "it's great, thanks", which is
useless.

> Could you answer these three, a line each?
> - What was the problem before?
> - What's different now?
> - Would you recommend it, and who to?

Three specific questions produce a usable quote. One vague question produces a
pleasantry.

---

## Converting to paid

At day 30, no pressure, just clarity:

> That's the 30 days done. It's handled [N] enquiries and [the result].
>
> Two options: keep it running with support and tuning at ₹[X] a month, or I hand it
> over documented and it keeps running without me. Genuinely happy either way — you've
> already given me what I asked for.

Some will pay. That is the actual goal — three free builds that produce one or two
paying retainers plus three case studies is a good month's work.

The ones who do not pay still owe you nothing. Hand over cleanly and stay friendly.
People move companies, and they remember who was straight with them.

---

## One thing to keep off the site

Do not advertise "we build free" publicly. It tells every future prospect your work is
worth nothing and that you have no clients.

Founding-client offers go out in direct conversation only. The website keeps its
prices.
