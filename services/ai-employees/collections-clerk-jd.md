## What this role owns

**Every unpaid invoice from the day it falls due until it is paid, disputed, or
handed to you.**

Nobody enjoys chasing money, which is exactly why it slips. This role does it on
time, in the same words every time, without anyone having to work up to it.

## What it does, in order

1. **Watches** your invoice ledger for anything past its due date.
2. **Checks whether it has already been paid** before every single send. This check
   is not optional and it is the most important thing in this document.
3. **Sends** a reminder appropriate to the age of the invoice, on the ladder you
   approved.
4. **Escalates in tone, not volume** — a nudge at seven days does not read like a
   nudge at sixty.
5. **Stops immediately** when payment lands, a dispute is raised, or a person replies
   with anything that is not "paying today".
6. **Tells you** who has gone quiet, who has promised and missed, and who needs a
   real conversation.
7. **Logs** every message sent against the invoice.

## The reconciliation check, and why it comes first

**Chasing a customer who has already paid is the single worst thing this role can
do.** It is embarrassing, it damages a relationship you spent money acquiring, and it
makes you look like you do not know your own books.

So: no reminder is sent without re-reading payment status at send time, not at queue
time. If the ledger is unreachable, unclear, or stale, **nothing sends and you are
told**. A missed reminder costs a day. A wrong one costs a customer.

If your payment data cannot support that check reliably, we will tell you this role
is not buildable yet, and what to fix first.

## What it escalates to a person

It stops and hands over — without sending — when:

- The customer disputes the invoice, the amount, or the work
- The customer says they have already paid
- Someone asks for a payment plan, an extension, or a discount
- A partial payment arrives
- The invoice is older than the last step on your ladder
- The account is one you flagged as sensitive
- The ledger is unreachable or the payment status is ambiguous

**Escalation is correct behaviour, not failure.** Anything involving a negotiation is
yours; this role handles the part that is just remembering.

## What it will never do

- **Take payment, move money, or confirm that a payment cleared.** It reads the
  ledger; it never writes to it.
- Threaten legal action, mention lawyers, courts, recovery agents or credit
  consequences — in any wording, at any invoice age
- Contact anyone other than the billing contact you named
- Add interest, penalties or fees it was not given
- Negotiate, settle, or agree to a plan
- Contact a customer whose payment status it could not verify
- Send outside working hours in the customer's timezone

**On tone:** the ladder escalates in firmness and never in aggression. A business that
owes you money is usually still a customer. We will not write anything you would be
uncomfortable signing yourself, and you approve every message on the ladder before it
is ever used.

## What we need from you

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ledger access** | Read access to whatever holds invoice status — Tally, Zoho, Refrens, QuickBooks or a sheet. **Verify the API exists on your plan before we quote.** "It has an API" and "we can use it on your plan" are different sentences. |
| **The ladder** | How many reminders, at what invoice ages, and in what words. We draft; you approve every line. |
| **Sensitive accounts** | Who must never be chased automatically. There is always a list, and forgetting to give it to us is how this goes wrong. |
| **A named manager** | One person who reads escalations and handles the negotiations. |
| **Baseline numbers** | Current average days-to-payment and the value currently overdue. Without these there is no way to show what changed. |

## Onboarding

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | Ledger access, ladder drafted, sensitive accounts listed, baseline recorded |
| **2** | Built and tested against your real overdue history — including invoices that were paid late, to confirm it would have stopped |
| **3** | **Shadow run.** It drafts every reminder, sends nothing. You read what would have gone out, to whom, and when. |
| **4** | Goes live on the oldest bucket only |
| **5–30** | Earlier buckets enabled as the reconciliation check proves itself |

Going live on the oldest bucket first is deliberate: those are the invoices where a
reminder is least likely to be a surprise.

## How performance is judged

Reviewed monthly against the baseline recorded on day one:

- Average days to payment, before and after
- Value collected, and value still outstanding by age
- Reminders sent, and reminders correctly withheld
- **Any reminder sent to someone who had already paid.** Target is zero; anything
  above zero is a defect, investigated and written up.
- Escalations, and whether the same reason keeps recurring

A monthly review document is issued whether or not you ask for it.

## Probation and ending it

**30 days.** At the end, either it stays at the agreed rate, or we hand over the
ladder and the logs and switch it off. Your ledger is untouched throughout — this
role only ever read from it.

## What this role is not

It is not a debt recovery service and it will not behave like one. It is the part of
your finance person's job that is remembering, being consistent, and being polite on
day sixty — so the conversations that need judgement still get a person.
