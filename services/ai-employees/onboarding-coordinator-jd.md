# Job description — Onboarding Coordinator

**Role:** Onboarding Coordinator (AI)
**Reports to:** _[named person on the client's side]_
**Starts:** 3–5 working days from access
**Cost:** ₹[   ] / month · setup ₹[   ] · cancel any time
**Probation:** 30 days

Send this before the build starts, not after. It is the scope document — it is what
stops the "why didn't it handle that" conversation in month two, and it is the thing
that makes an AI employee feel like a hire rather than a subscription.

---

## What this role owns

**Everything between "they said yes" and "the work can actually start."**

That gap is where new clients form their opinion of you, and it is almost always run
from somebody's memory. This role makes it happen the same way every time.

## What it does, in order

1. **Notices** a deal marked won, in your CRM or wherever you actually record it.
2. **Sends the welcome** — the real one, with names, next steps and dates, not a
   template with a merge field left showing.
3. **Creates** what needs to exist: the folder, the workspace, the project, the
   channel, the tracker row.
4. **Requests** every document and detail you need, in one clear list rather than
   five separate emails.
5. **Chases** what has not arrived, on a schedule, and stops the moment it does.
6. **Books** the kickoff against the right people's calendars.
7. **Tells you when it is stuck** — a client gone quiet, a document refused, a detail
   that contradicts what was sold.
8. **Hands over** a clean, complete file to whoever does the work.

## What it escalates to a person

It stops and hands over when:

- The client asks a question about scope, price or the contract
- Something they send contradicts what was sold
- A required document is refused, or arrives in a form nobody can use
- The client goes quiet past the point your chase ladder covers
- Anyone sounds unhappy, confused or surprised
- It cannot tell whether a requirement has actually been met

**Escalation is correct behaviour, not failure.** A new client who is confused in
week one is the most expensive escalation you will get and the cheapest to fix — it
should reach a person immediately, not after three more automated emails.

## What it will never do

- Answer a question about scope, price, contract or deliverables
- Promise a date, a person or an outcome it was not explicitly given
- **Send, request or store credentials.** Access requests point at your password
  manager; nothing sensitive passes through a chat or an email body.
- Mark a step complete that it cannot verify
- Keep chasing someone who has said they are struggling or asked for time
- Start the work, or tell a client the work has started

## What we need from you

| | |
|---|---|
| **A reliable "won" signal** | One place a deal is recorded as won, that people actually update. If deals are marked won three days late, this role starts three days late. |
| **The checklist** | Every document, detail and access you need before work begins — written down, probably for the first time. |
| **Tool access** | Whatever you create per client: Drive, Notion, Slack, your project tool. |
| **The chase ladder** | How many times, how far apart, and when it stops and becomes a phone call. |
| **A named manager** | One person who picks up escalations and takes the awkward conversations. |
| **Baseline numbers** | How long onboarding takes today, start to kickoff, and how often something is missing at kickoff. |

Writing the checklist down is most of the value, and it is work only you can do. It
is common to discover during this that two people on your team onboard clients
differently — that is worth knowing on its own.

## Onboarding

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1** | Access, checklist agreed, chase ladder approved, baseline recorded |
| **2–3** | Built and tested against your last five real onboardings |
| **4–5** | **Shadow run.** Every message drafted, nothing sent, nothing created. You read the whole sequence as a client would receive it. |
| **Go live** | On new deals only — never retrofitted onto clients already mid-onboarding |
| **6–30** | Chase ladder and checklist tuned as real clients move through |

**It is never pointed at an onboarding already in progress.** A client who has
already had three emails from a person should not suddenly start getting them from a
system.

## How performance is judged

Reviewed monthly against the baseline recorded on day one:

- Days from deal won to kickoff held
- Share of kickoffs where everything needed was already in hand
- Documents chased, and how many chases each took
- Escalations, and whether the same reason keeps recurring
- Hours returned to whoever used to run this from memory

A monthly review document is issued whether or not you ask for it.

## Probation and ending it

**30 days.** At the end, either it stays at the agreed rate, or we hand over the
checklist, the templates and the ladder and switch it off. Everything it created
lives in your own tools and stays there.

## What this role is not

It is not an account manager and it does not have a relationship with your client. It
does the part that is chasing, creating and remembering — so the person who owns the
relationship spends their time on the relationship.
